## Process check

- **Notice:** The letter states the facts, the provision, the requested action, and a deadline. Delivery method and deadline length are placeholders pending the documents.
- **Opportunity to cure:** Included. The trailer can be removed by the deadline, or the owner can show an exception or approval.
- **Hearing right:** Flagged, not assumed. The letter offers a chance to respond and to be heard. Fill in the exact mechanism if the documents or statute require one before any fine.
- **Consequence:** Stated only as "the Board may proceed as [Section __] provides," with a promise of further written notice. No threat, no invented amount.
- **One matter per letter:** Yes. Do not add other violations to this notice.
- **Facts to verify before sending:**
  - Who observed the trailer, and when, with dated photos if possible.
  - That the property address and owner of record are correct.
  - Whether a tenant occupies the home (the placeholder cc line covers this).
  - Whether the Board or manager has ever approved or tolerated this trailer.
- **Consistency:** Confirm the Association has enforced this provision against similar trailers or vehicles. Selective enforcement is the most common defense to a violation notice. Also confirm the Board authorized the notice, if the documents or Association practice require it.
- **Tone check:** The letter contains no accusation, no characterization of the owner, and no unsupported claim. The draft states what was seen, what the documents say, and what the owner can do.

## Counsel flag

**Not required for this notice** if the following hold:
- It is a first courtesy notice.
- It cites a clear provision.
- It offers a cure and a response opportunity.
- It contains no fine.

**Send it to the Association's attorney first if any of these apply:**
- The provision is ambiguous about driveways or trailers.
- The owner has raised a defense, such as prior approval, a grandfathered use, or a claimed exception.
- The documents or state law impose specific notice, hearing, or fine procedures you have not verified.
- The Association has enforced this rule inconsistently.
- The matter is likely to escalate to a fine, a lien, or litigation.

This is correspondence, not legal advice.

## Push-back worth hearing

- The request assumes the trailer violates the rules. Many communities restrict trailers only when visible from the street, or allow limited driveway parking. Read the actual provision before sending. If the rule doesn't clearly reach a driveway, this letter should not go out as written.
- "Ask them to remove it" implies a courtesy notice. If the documents require a formal first notice, or a hearing before any escalation, use that format instead. Sending a friendly letter that doesn't satisfy the required notice can restart the clock later.
