# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Argue with the work, not the person. Assume the other party is trying.

## Scope

This applies in the issue tracker, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, and any other space
that belongs to this project, plus anywhere someone is representing the project in public.

## What is expected

- **Criticize the artifact.** "This benchmark does not support that claim" is exactly the contribution
  this project wants. "You are incompetent" is not the same sentence and does not carry the same
  information.
- **Be specific enough to be checkable.** A disagreement with a file and a line in it can be settled.
  One without cannot.
- **Take the correction.** When you are shown to be wrong, say so and move on. This repo's own
  documentation retires its own headline claim, so nobody here gets to treat being wrong as an
  insult.
- **Respect a "no."** A maintainer declining a feature is a scoping decision, not a personal one.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, whether public or private. Sexual attention or advances of any kind.
- Insults, slurs, or demeaning comments, including about someone's identity, nationality, background,
  experience level, or the tooling they use.
- Publishing anyone's private information without their explicit permission.
- Sustained disruption: reopening settled decisions to wear people down, or escalating a technical
  disagreement into a personal one.
- Using an issue or PR to advertise something unrelated.

**AI-assisted contributions are welcome and do not need a disclaimer.** What is not acceptable is
submitting output you have not read. If you cannot answer a question about a diff you opened, that is
the problem, not the tool that produced it.

## Reporting

Report a concern through
[GitHub's private reporting form](https://github.com/emtcmca/guildproof/security/advisories/new),
which is private until published, or by opening an issue titled `conduct contact request` with no
detail in it, and you will be given somewhere private to write.

**Be aware of who you are reporting to.** This project has a single maintainer, so a report about the
maintainer goes to the maintainer. That is a real limitation and worth stating rather than papering
over: if that is not acceptable for your situation, GitHub's own
[abuse reporting](https://github.com/contact/report-abuse) is independent of this repo and is the
right channel.

Reports are handled privately. The reporter's identity is not shared without their consent.

## Consequences

The maintainer may edit or delete comments, close issues or PRs, and block accounts, in proportion to
what happened and with a stated reason. A first, isolated lapse usually gets a warning. Harassment
and deliberate disruption do not need one.

## Attribution

of the [Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org) v2.1 without inheriting its text,
so the wording above is the actual policy and there is no second document to consult.
